SideDeck

Your day, on the side.

A small always-on dock for macOS. Everything you keep checking, at the edge of the screen.

macOS 14 and later, Apple silicon and Intel. Free and open source.

That dock is running. The clock is your clock.

One task, counting down

The top card is whatever you are working on. Press the bolt and the ring empties as the time goes; at zero the task is done and the next moves up.

The time, without the menu bar

Figures or a dial, as two separate widgets, so you keep the one you actually read. Seconds and the date are optional.

Battery and Wi‑Fi, in one outline

The charge and whether you are online, inside an outline that is the battery too: it fills and runs down around the figure, blue while it charges. Put the percentage or the symbol away and it closes into a ring.

Six weeks, at a glance

Every day you finish something fills a dot, brighter the more you did. Thirty-six of them: long enough to see a streak, short enough to still care.

A glass of water, on a timer

The glass drains as the clock runs down and fills when you drink. Water, not a progress bar: it pours, it sloshes, it settles. Leave it empty and the card glows at you.

The three lines you keep re-reading

Notes and sticky paper on the dock, not in an app you have to open. Click a card, write, click away. It is saved.
